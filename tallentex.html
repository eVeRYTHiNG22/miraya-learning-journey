Where the 320 marks are

Five sections, in the order they're printed. Open a section to see Allen's official 2027 topic list for it — this list, not the whole NCERT book, is what can be asked. (9th) and (10th) are the class each topic belongs to.

Two kinds of chapter are in that table. Most are the later Class-10 chapters an October exam can't reach — neither real 2024 paper asked a single question from them. A few — Circles, Probability, Diversity in Living Organisms, Why Do We Fall Ill, Natural Resources — were asked in 2024 but have been dropped from the 2027 list, so when you practise from old papers, skip those questions. Note the class labels too: Allen lists Statistics and Surface Areas & Volumes as Class 9 only, so the Class-10 chapters of the same names aren't on the list either.
Class 9 is not "revision" here. In the 2024 papers, 10 of the 15 Physics questions and about 6 of the 15 Biology questions were Class-9 material — close to half of all the subject questions.

How to play it

TALLENTEX is a time-pressure test, not a knowledge test. A strong student clears only about half the questions in under a minute; the rest run past their 90-second share — so the marks go to whoever manages the clock and the negative marking best. Six rules.

1Never leave a question blank

Negative marking makes most students over-skip. The maths says the opposite. A wrong answer costs 1; a right one earns 4. So an attempt breaks even when you're right just 1 time in 5 — and a pure blind guess between four options is right 1 time in 4. Even a blind guess is worth more than a blank, and every option you can rule out makes it worth much more.

One honest caveat. "Average" means over many questions — any single guess can still lose a mark. And on a puzzle whose rule is never actually stated, a confident wrong pattern can score below random. Still attempt it — treat it as a pure guess rather than a pattern you trust, and don't sink minutes into it.

2Don't sit the paper in printed order

Biology is mostly recall and the fastest section; Chemistry and Physics are next; Mental Ability and Maths hold most of the long calculations. The printed order puts Maths fourth and the 80-mark Mental Ability section last — and Maths is the one section where a single question can swallow four minutes. Sat front-to-back, it eats the time the biggest section needs, and unattempted easy marks are the most expensive kind. Put the slowest section last, where it can only spend what's left.

3Ten seconds to decide, ninety to act

80 questions in 120 minutes is 90 seconds each, and a fair number of them need two to four minutes. You can't give every question its full 90. So in the first ten seconds of reading, sort it:

  • Solve — you see the method. Do it now.
  • Eliminate, guess, move on — you can rule out one or two options but the rest would take minutes. Take the guess (rule 1) and flag it.
  • Skip and return — no idea yet. Mark it in the booklet, come back with leftover time. Before the bell, guess every skipped one.

4No calculator, no rough paper

Calculators, log tables and blank paper are all banned in the hall; rough work goes on the booklet's rough-work pages. IGCSE has trained you to reach for a calculator — for this exam, practise everything by hand from today: fraction arithmetic, square roots to one decimal, and the ratio shortcuts. When two options differ only in the second decimal place, the arithmetic is the question.

5The multi-statement trap

TALLENTEX's signature trap hands you four to twelve statements — "three students said the following…" — and asks which are correct. That is four to twelve tiny questions inside one 4-mark answer, and a single misread clause flips the whole thing from +4 to −1: a five-mark swing. Read every statement to its last word before you touch the options. Never pick an option because its first statement looks right.

6OMR discipline

Answers go on a machine-read sheet in pen and can't be changed once darkened. Bubble in batches of five, and read the question number aloud in your head each time — one slipped row mis-marks every question after it. Keep the last four minutes for checking the sheet against the booklet.
