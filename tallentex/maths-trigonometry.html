Trigonometry

Official topic: Introduction to Trigonometry (Class 10). Most of this chapter is new to you — your IGCSE course teaches sin, cos, tan and the exact values, but it has no sec, cosec or cot and no identities at all. That missing half is precisely what TALLENTEX asks. Read this once now and once the night before.

What the paper actually asks here

Every trigonometry question in the five cached real papers was pulled out and re-solved against Allen's own answer key — including the 2017 and 2018 booklets, which are scans and do not turn up in a text search. Eight items, and they agree almost eerily:

That is one or two of the fifteen Maths questions in every single paper. Four things follow, and they should change how you revise:

  1. Not one of the eight has a figure. TALLENTEX trigonometry is algebra. There is no triangle to measure and nothing to read off a diagram.
  2. Not one of them asks you to find an angle. θ is never the answer. You are always asked for the value of an expression.
  3. Heights and Distances is not on the 2027 syllabus at all. No ladders, no towers, no angles of elevation. Do not spend a minute on it.
  4. Allen recycles the same question. Look at 2017 Q57 and 2024 Q56: same shape, tiny change of constants, and both answers are 4. Seven years apart. Learn the machine in section 6 and you have very likely already done the 2027 version of it.

Six of the eight are the same machine: you are handed one equation linking the ratios, you squeeze it down to a single number or a single polynomial, and the thing you are asked for turns out to be built from exactly that. Sections 4–6 are that machine, taken apart. (The two exceptions: 2018 Q56 is a straight exact-values substitution — section 2 — and 2018 Q57 asks you to eliminate a second angle between two relations, which is the one genuinely different shape in seven years of papers.)

1The six ratios — three you know, three you don't

NCERT names the sides of a right-angled triangle relative to the angle you are looking at: P perpendicular (opposite), B base (adjacent), H hypotenuse (always the side facing the right angle). Get this labelling right and the rest is bookkeeping.

The three you already have

sin A = P/H  ·  cos A = B/H  ·  tan A = P/B = sin A / cos A

Identical to the SOH-CAH-TOA you use in IGCSE — only the letters are NCERT's.

The three that are new to you

Each is simply one of the first three turned upside down. There is nothing else to them.

cosec A = H/P = 1/sin A
Pairs with sin. Note the spelling: cosec, and it is the reciprocal of sin — not of cos, despite the "co".
sec A = H/B = 1/cos A
Pairs with cos. sec goes with cos — the one without the "co" in its name.
cot A = B/P = 1/tan A = cos A / sin A
Pairs with tan. Also equal to cos/sin, which is the form that turns up most often inside an expression.

The pairing trap — worth ten seconds of memorising

The obvious guess is that sec pairs with sin and cosec with cos, because the names look like they match. It is the other way round. The reliable rule:

Exactly one of the two names in a reciprocal pair carries "co". sin ↔ cosec. cos ↔ sec. tan ↔ cot. If you ever write a pair with two "co"s or none, it is wrong.

2Exact values — no calculator, so these must be automatic

You already know the sin/cos/tan row from IGCSE. What is new is the bottom three rows — and the fact that in TALLENTEX there is no calculator, no log table and no rough paper. These thirty numbers have to come out of your head in under a second each.

How to rebuild the table in fifteen seconds

Write 0   1   2   3   4. Take the square root of each and halve it — that is the whole sin row:

√0/2 = 0  ·  √1/2 = 1/2  ·  √2/2 = 1/√2  ·  √3/2  ·  √4/2 = 1

The cos row is the same five numbers read backwards. The tan row is sin ÷ cos. The bottom three rows are the top three flipped. You never memorise thirty numbers — you memorise five and a direction.

Where "not defined" bites

tan 90° and sec 90° are not defined because cos 90° = 0 and you cannot divide by zero. Likewise cot 0° and cosec 0°, because sin 0° = 0. Write "not defined" — never ∞, and never 0. An option offering "0" for tan 90° is there to catch exactly that.

3The three identities — this is the new half of the chapter

Your IGCSE syllabus contains no trigonometric identities at all. These three lines are the single most valuable thing on this page: every one of the four real questions in the table above is solved with them.

All three are Pythagoras in disguise. Start with p² + b² = h² and divide the whole line by the square of each of the three sides in turn:

Divide p² + b² = h² by…You getWhich reads
h²(p/h)² + (b/h)² = 1sin²A + cos²A = 1
b²(p/b)² + 1 = (h/b)²1 + tan²A = sec²A
p²1 + (b/p)² = (h/p)²1 + cot²A = cosec²A

So there is really only one identity, met three times. If you ever blank, write Pythagoras down and divide.

The rearranged forms — memorise these, not the originals

The paper almost never hands you an identity the right way up. It hands you a fragment and expects you to recognise it. These are the forms that actually appear:

sec²A − tan²A = 1
So sec²A − 1 = tan²A and tan²A + 1 = sec²A. Sample Q52 is nothing but spotting sec²A − 1 and writing tan²A.
cosec²A − cot²A = 1
So cosec²A − 1 = cot²A. The same move, one row down.
sin²A = 1 − cos²A
And cos²A = 1 − sin²A. This is how you turn a mixed equation into one containing a single ratio — see section 5.

The conjugate trick — the highest-value line on this page

Because sec²A − tan²A = 1 is a difference of two squares, it factorises:

(sec A + tan A)(sec A − tan A) = 1

Which means sec A + tan A and sec A − tan A are reciprocals of each other. Hand one to a student and they have the other for free. The same holds one row down:

(cosec A + cot A)(cosec A − cot A) = 1

Whenever a question gives you sec θ + tan θ = k, immediately write sec θ − tan θ = 1/k. Adding the two lines gives you 2 sec θ; subtracting them gives you 2 tan θ. Two lines of work and the question is finished — worked example 3 does exactly this.

Notation: sin²A means (sin A)², not sin(A²)

sin²A = sin A × sin A. It is written with the square on the "sin" purely to avoid a forest of brackets. sin(A²) is a completely different and entirely useless object — it will never appear. Similarly sin⁻¹A is not 1/sin A — that is cosec A. It means "the angle whose sine is A", so sin⁻¹(1/2) = 30°.

4Technique 1 — one ratio given, all six wanted

The commonest opening move in the whole chapter. You are told one ratio and asked for an expression built from several. Do not reach for an identity. Draw the triangle.

Suppose you are told sec θ = 13/5 and asked for tan θ + cot θ.

From the triangle: tan θ = 12/5 and cot θ = 5/12, so

tan θ + cot θ = 12/5 + 5/12 = 144/60 + 25/60 = 169/60

Learn the three Pythagorean triples by sight

Examiners reuse them endlessly because they keep the arithmetic calculator-free:

3, 4, 5  ·  5, 12, 13  ·  8, 15, 17

and their multiples (6-8-10, 9-12-15, 10-24-26 …). If a question hands you 5/13 or 8/17, the missing side is already known to you — that is a free five seconds.

The identity route, for when the triangle is awkward

The same answer falls out algebraically, and it is worth seeing because the paper sometimes wants the general form rather than a number:

tan θ + cot θ = sin θ/cos θ + cos θ/sin θ = (sin²θ + cos²θ)/(sin θ cos θ) = 1/(sin θ cos θ)

Check it: sin θ = 12/13, cos θ = 5/13, so 1/(sin θ cos θ) = 169/60 ✓ — the same number by a different road. Two routes agreeing is the cheapest error-check you own; use it whenever you have thirty spare seconds.

5Technique 2 — a mixed equation becomes a quadratic

When the given equation contains both sin and cos, and one of them is squared, you can always force it into a quadratic in a single ratio. This is the engine behind 2024 code A, Q56.

Worked: if 2 sin²θ = 3 cos θ and θ is acute, find cos θ.

  1. Pick the survivor. The unsquared ratio is cos, so aim for an equation in cos alone.
  2. Replace the squared one using sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ:
    2(1 − cos²θ) = 3 cos θ
  3. Tidy into standard form. Write c for cos θ:
    2 − 2c² = 3c  →  2c² + 3c − 2 = 0
  4. Factorise: (2c − 1)(c + 2) = 0  →  c = 1/2 or c = −2
  5. Throw out the impossible root. A cosine can never be less than −1, so c = −2 is rejected. cos θ = 1/2, i.e. θ = 60°.

Step 5 is not optional, and it is where the marks go

A quadratic hands you two roots and the paper will always list the rejected one as an option. sin θ and cos θ live between −1 and 1; for an acute angle they live between 0 and 1. Anything outside that is not an answer, it is a trap. Check the range before you look at the options, not after — once you have seen your number printed as option (4) it is very hard to talk yourself out of it.

(tan and cot have no such limit — they run over every real number. sec and cosec are the opposite way round: they can never lie strictly between −1 and 1.)

Same machine, uglier numbers

2024 code A, Q56 gives you 8 tan θ = 3 cos θ. Write tan as sin/cos, multiply up, replace cos²θ by 1 − sin²θ, and you have a quadratic in sin θ — then all six ratios follow from the triangle, exactly as in section 4. But read its demand to the end: it does not want E, it wants E written as (a + b√c)/d and then the value of a + b + c + d. Working E out and bubbling it scores −1, because E itself is not one of the options. That question will turn up in a mock later in the programme, so it is deliberately not solved here. The method above is all you need to beat it.

6Technique 3 — the expression is the identity

The hardest-looking trigonometry in these papers is also the most mechanical once you see the shape. You are given a monstrous expression and asked what it equals. The move is always the same: factor out, then use sin² + cos² = 1 to collapse a bracket to 1.

Worked — the official sample paper, Q58. If Tn = sinⁿθ + cosⁿθ, find (T₃ − T₅) / T₁.

Write the numerator out and group the sines with the sines:

T₃ − T₅ = (sin³θ + cos³θ) − (sin⁵θ + cos⁵θ) = sin³θ(1 − sin²θ) + cos³θ(1 − cos²θ)

Now each bracket collapses — 1 − sin²θ = cos²θ and 1 − cos²θ = sin²θ:

= sin³θ·cos²θ + cos³θ·sin²θ = sin²θ cos²θ (sin θ + cos θ) = sin²θ cos²θ · T₁

So (T₃ − T₅)/T₁ = sin²θ cos²θ. The question asks which of the listed expressions equals it — and (T₅ − T₇)/T₃ collapses to exactly the same thing by exactly the same two lines. Official key: option (1).

The pattern to carry into the exam

Any time you see a difference of two powers of sin and cos, the lower power factors out and what is left is 1 − (something)², which is the other ratio squared. You are never really doing trigonometry — you are doing algebra with sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 as the only fact.

2024 code U, Q56 is the same idea run backwards: a given cubic in sin θ is rearranged into a cubic in cos²θ, and the expression you are asked for turns out to be that very cubic. It too is being saved for a later mock, so it is not worked here.

7Complementary angles — a free shortcut

Two acute angles of a right triangle add to 90°. Stand at one, then the other, and P and B swap — which is the picture at the very top of this page. Every complementary rule is just that swap written down.

sin(90° − A) = cos A
and cos(90° − A) = sin A
tan(90° − A) = cot A
and cot(90° − A) = tan A
sec(90° − A) = cosec A
and cosec(90° − A) = sec A

The rule in words: the angle turns into its complement and the name swaps with its "co" partner. So sin 55° = cos 35°, because 55 + 35 = 90.

What this is for

It turns a fraction that looks impossible without a calculator into the number 1:

sin 25° / cos 65° = sin 25° / sin 25° = 1

Whenever you see two angles in one expression that add to 90, this is why they are there. Scan for that sum first — it is usually the entire question.

Three errors this chapter punishes hardest

  • Answering the wrong ratio. You correctly find θ = 30° and then read off sin instead of cot. Underline what is being asked before you start, and check it again before you bubble — this is the single most expensive slip in the section.
  • (sin A + cos A)² is not sin²A + cos²A. It is sin²A + cos²A + 2 sin A cos A = 1 + 2 sin A cos A. The cross term is deliberately left out of one option in nearly every paper that asks this.
  • Flipping a reciprocal one time too many or too few. If you want cot and you have tan = 3/4, cot is 4/3 — not 3/4, and not −3/4. Say the word "flip" out loud as you do it.

8Five worked questions — at TALLENTEX pitch

One per technique, in the order you met them. Try each before opening the working, and give yourself 90 seconds — that is the real paper's average. The elimination reasoning is written out because on the day, ruling two options out is worth almost as much as solving.

1One ratio → all six · ~70 seconds

If 15 cot A = 8 and A is acute, then (sin A + cos A) / (sin A − cos A) equals

Working

15 cot A = 8 → cot A = 8/15. cot is B/P, so B = 8, P = 15, and H = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17 — the 8-15-17 triple.

Read the two ratios off: sin A = 15/17, cos A = 8/17.

(15/17 + 8/17) / (15/17 − 8/17) = (23/17) / (7/17) = 23/7 — the 17s cancel, which is why the answer is a clean fraction and why you should never convert to decimals.

Where each wrong option comes from. (1) −23/7 — reading cot A = 8/15 as tan A = 8/15. That swaps P and B, giving sin A = 8/17 and cos A = 15/17, so the denominator turns negative: 23/(−7). (3) 7/23 — the right two ratios, but the final fraction written upside down. (2) −7/23 — both slips at once. Notice that all four options are ± the same two numbers: that is the examiner telling you the arithmetic is easy and the bookkeeping is the test. Elimination: cot A = 8/15 is less than 1, so tan A > 1 and A is past 45° — which makes sin A bigger than cos A and the denominator positive. Both negative options die before you compute a single thing.

Elimination: option (4) is the only one still carrying θ, and the numerator and denominator plainly share the same factor, so the answer must be a pure number — (4) can be struck without any algebra at all.

Working

Because sec²θ − tan²θ = 1, the conjugate is the reciprocal: sec θ − tan θ = 1/3.

Add the two equations: 2 sec θ = 3 + 1/3 = 10/3 → sec θ = 5/3 → cos θ = 3/5.

Subtract them: 2 tan θ = 3 − 1/3 = 8/3 → tan θ = 4/3.

sin θ = tan θ × cos θ = (4/3)(3/5) = 4/5. Check: sec θ + tan θ = 5/3 + 4/3 = 3 ✓

(Faster still: sec θ = 5/3 means H = 5, B = 3, so P = 4 — the 3-4-5 triangle — and sin θ = 4/5 straight off.)

Where each wrong option comes from. Every wrong option is a real number you produce on the way: (4) 5/3 is sec θ, (3) 4/3 is tan θ, (1) 3/5 is cos θ. Not one of them is filler. That is the designed trap — the working is easy and the only difficulty is remembering which of four numbers on your page the question actually wanted. Underline the word "sin" in the stem before you start. Elimination: a sine can never exceed 1, so (3) 4/3 and (4) 5/3 are impossible answers no matter how naturally the working produces them. The real choice was only ever between 3/5 and 4/5 — which is a 1-in-2 guess worth +1.5 marks on average if you stall.

5Quadratic, rejected root, then a chain · ~90 seconds

If 5 cos²θ = 7 sin θ − 1 and 0° < θ < 90°, then cot θ equals

13/4
24/5

Working

Section 5's machine again, but the constants are different and there is a stray − 1 on the right, so nothing can be copied across — you have to build the quadratic yourself.

The unsquared ratio is sin, so aim for sin. Replace cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ, and write s for sin θ:

5(1 − s²) = 7s − 1 → 5 − 5s² = 7s − 1 → 5s² + 7s − 6 = 0

Factorise: (5s − 3)(s + 2) = 0, so s = 3/5 or s = −2. Reject −2: a sine can never be below −1. Hence sin θ = 3/5.

P = 3, H = 5, so B = 4 — the 3-4-5 triangle — and cot θ = B/P = 4/3.

Where each wrong option comes from. All three belong to a student who did the hard part perfectly and then read the wrong ratio off the triangle: (1) 3/4 is tan θ — the reciprocal flipped the wrong way; (2) 4/5 is cos θ; (4) 5/3 is cosec θ. The algebra is not what this question tests. Finding sin θ is the first half of the work, not the end of it — and in a paper marked +4/−1, stopping early costs you 5 marks against the student who did not. Elimination: sin θ = 3/5 puts θ below 45°, where tan θ < 1 and so cot θ > 1. That strikes (1) and (2) on sight, leaving a two-way choice worth +1.5 marks on average even if the last step deserts you.
