Mensuration & Heron’s Formula

Official topics: Heron’s Formula and Surface area and Volumes (both Class 9) — two topics, one card. Your IGCSE course already has the solids — cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere and their surface areas and volumes — so that half is a formula check plus the five ways Allen dresses them up. Heron’s formula is new: the area of a triangle from its three sides alone, and the surd arithmetic it drags in when there is no calculator. A 30-minute read, then the paper.

What that tells you:

  1. One or two of the fifteen Maths questions a sitting — 2017-E two, 2018-S two, 2024-U one, 2024-A two, the sample two — so 4–8 marks, plus whatever the neighbours add. Both 2024 codes put the solids question at Q51; Heron sat at Q53 in 2017 and 2018 and at Q49 in 2024-A.
  2. None of the nine carries a figure. You are given three numbers and you draw the triangle or the solid yourself — so the first skill is knowing which faces and which lengths exist before any arithmetic starts. (The two 2024-A neighbours do come with figures.)
  3. No Heron item is a bare "apply the formula". Every one chains: ratio → sides → area (and the fast route is to notice the right angle and skip Heron, 2017-E); area → altitude (2018-S); area given → find the sides (2024-A, where the honest algebra is a quadratic and the fast route is to test the options); area → sixteen tiles → rupees (Sample). Section 2 has all four.
  4. The solids items are five different machines, not five formulas. Two are pure ratio (a length ratio k gives areas k² and volumes k³ — Sample Q56, 2024-A Q51); one adds the faces a cut exposes (2024-U Q51); one compares three solids on one base (2018-S Q54); one enumerates an integer parameter (2017-E Q54). π never survived to an answer in any of the five — it cancelled in the ratios and was absent from the box — so leave it as a symbol until you are sure it stays.
  5. Allen borrows NCERT's exercise numbers. Sample Q55 is NCERT's floral-design exercise — 9, 28, 35, sixteen tiles, 50 paise — reproduced, with √6 = 2.45 supplied; 2017-E Q53 is the shape of NCERT's ratio-and-perimeter exercise. (Both recognised from memory of the textbook — no NCERT copy is cached in this project.) If a stem looks familiar, it probably is: trust the memory, but still do the arithmetic.
  6. The hardest two are the ones where you must know what a cut creates or what a solid's total surface includes — 2024-U Q51 (a cut exposes two circles, each of radius √(R² − x²)) and 2018-S Q54 (a hemisphere's total surface is 3πr², a cone's needs its slant height). Sections 3 and 4 are built around exactly those two facts.

Budget accordingly. A 4–8-mark unit whose items take 60–150 s each once the machine is recognised. The marks are lost on the set-up — which faces, which height, which formula needs the slant height — not on the multiplication.

1Heron’s formula — area from three sides, and the surd it leaves behind

Your IGCSE course finds a triangle's area with ½ × base × height or ½ab sin C. NCERT Class 9 adds a third way that needs neither a height nor an angle — only the three sides. Allen uses it because it produces a square root you must simplify by hand.

Definition · NCERT Class 9

For a triangle with sides a, b, c, the semi-perimeter is s = (a + b + c)/2, and

Area = √[ s (s − a) (s − b) (s − c) ]

Heron (Hero) of Alexandria stated it in his Metrica; NCERT credits him and names the chapter after him. It works for every triangle — scalene, isosceles, right-angled — but it is the slow route whenever a height is easy to get.

The 90-second routine (no calculator)

  1. s first, then the three brackets. Write s, s − a, s − b, s − c on one line. Check: the three brackets sum to s. If any bracket is zero or negative the triangle does not exist (that is the triangle inequality in disguise).
  2. Do not multiply out. Factorise each of the four numbers into primes and pair the factors: every pair comes out of the root, every unpaired prime stays under it.
  3. Approximate what is left only if the options are decimals. √105 is "just over 10" — and 10.25² = 105.06, which is how 2018-S Q53's option (2) was set.

Run it on the 2018-S triangle, sides 11, 12, 13: s = 18; brackets 7, 6, 5 (sum 18 ✓). Product 18 × 7 × 6 × 5 — as primes, (2·3²) · 7 · (2·3) · 5 = 2² · 3³ · 5 · 7. Pairs: one 2-pair and one 3-pair come out as 2 × 3 = 6; left under the root: 3 × 5 × 7 = 105. So Area = 6√105 ≈ 6 × 10.25 ≈ 61.5 cm². Multiplying out to 3780 and then hunting for its square root is the slow way and the error-prone way.

All three are Heron's formula in disguise — NCERT proves the equilateral one from it — but each saves a minute. Reading "5 : 12 : 13" and reaching for Heron is the mark of a student who has not looked at the numbers.

Traps · the three that cost the marks

  • Using the perimeter for s. s is half the perimeter. With the whole perimeter in place of s every bracket changes and the "area" is nonsense — but it is a number, and a setter can list it.
  • Stopping before the square root, or dropping what stays under it. s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c) is the square of the area: for 11-12-13 that product is 3780, the area is 6√105 ≈ 61.5. The subtler version is pairing the primes correctly and then forgetting the unpaired ones — writing "6" for 6√105 — which produces a clean whole number that is wrong by a factor of ten. The reverse slip (halving a Heron answer "because ½bh has a half") is a third option a setter can print.
  • Mixed units. A perimeter in metres with sides in centimetres, or a rate in paise with an answer in rupees (Sample Q55: 50 paise/cm² on 1411.2 cm² is ₹705.6, not ₹70,560).

2The four Heron machines the real papers use

Nine real items, and not one says "find the area of a triangle with sides a, b, c". Each one puts Heron in the middle of a chain. Learn the chains, not the formula.

A · Ratio and perimeter → the sides → the area (2017-E Q53)

Sides in the ratio p : q : r with perimeter P: the sides are pk, qk, rk with k = P/(p + q + r). 5 : 12 : 13 and 450 m → k = 450/30 = 15 → 75, 180, 195. Then look before you leap: 5-12-13 is a right triangle, so the area is ½ × 75 × 180 = 6750 m² and Heron is never needed. If the ratio had been, say, 4 : 5 : 6 you would have to run Heron — that is the NCERT exercise shape, and it is the version a setter uses when the right-angle shortcut is not meant to exist.

B · Area → an altitude (2018-S Q53)

Once you have the area by Heron, every altitude follows from ½bh: h = 2 × Area / base. The altitude to the 12 cm side of the 11-12-13 triangle is 2 × 6√105 / 12 = √105 ≈ 10.25 cm. The paper offered 10.5, 10.25, 10 and 7.25: 10.25² = 105.06 settles it without a calculator, and 10 (10² = 100) is what a student who rounds √105 too early bubbles.

Which altitude is shortest? The one to the longest side — h = 2A/b falls as b grows. Allen's "altitude corresponding to the side of length…" always names the side; read which.

C · Area given → find the sides (2024-A Q49)

"AB = (x − 2), BC = (x + 2), AC = x, area 2x√(x/2); the sides are…" The honest route squares Heron: s = 3x/2, brackets x/2 + 2, x/2 − 2, x/2, so Area² = (3x²/4)(x²/4 − 4) = 3x²(x² − 16)/16; setting that equal to (2x√(x/2))² = 2x³ gives 3x² − 32x − 48 = 0, a quadratic — and Quadratic Equations is not on the 2027 list. So do what the options invite: test them. The middle option (3) is 10, 12, 14: s = 18, brackets 8, 6, 4, product 18 × 8 × 6 × 4 = 3456 = 24² × 6, area 24√6; and 2x√(x/2) at x = 12 is 24 × √6. ✓ One test, done. (For the record, (3x + 4)(x − 12) = 0 gives x = 12 too.)

Exam tactic · when the options are the answer, test them

If the four options are four triples of sides, the question is asking you to check, not to solve. Start from the option in the middle of the range, run the 90-second routine on it, and you are finished in under a minute — while the student setting up the quadratic is still expanding brackets. The same trick works on "which value cannot be C" (2017-E Q54, section 4).

D · Area → many copies → a cost (Sample Q55)

Sides 9, 28, 35: s = 36, brackets 27, 8, 1 (sum 36 ✓), product 36 × 27 × 8 = 2² · 3² · 3³ · 2³ = 2⁵ · 3⁵, which is (2² · 3²)² × 6 = 36² × 6 → Area = 36√6 ≈ 36 × 2.45 = 88.2 cm². Sixteen tiles: 1411.2 cm². At 50 paise per cm²: ₹705.6. The traps are the two unit conversions (paise → rupees; 16 tiles, not one) and the given √6 — use the value Allen prints, not your own, or you land between two options.

E · A quadrilateral — split it along a diagonal

NCERT removed the "quadrilaterals by Heron" section in its 2023 rationalisation (from memory of the textbook, as with the note in section 3), but the machine is one line and Allen's subject-level items use it: draw a diagonal, find the two triangle areas, add. If one of the triangles has a right angle you get its area from ½bh and the diagonal from Pythagoras, which then feeds Heron on the other triangle.

4The five solids machines the real papers use

Five real items, five different shapes of question. In every one the formula is the easy part; the mark is for knowing what to apply it to.

A · Ratio items — never compute either solid (Sample Q56, 2024-A Q51)

If every length scales by k, every area scales by k² and every volume by k³ — whatever the solid, whatever the formula. Spheres with radii 4 : 5 have volumes 4³ : 5³ = 64 : 125 (the option 16 : 25 is the surface ratio). A hemispherical balloon blown from 6 cm to 12 cm has k = 2, so its surface area goes up 4× — 1 : 4 — and it makes no difference whether "surface area" means the curved 2πr² or the total 3πr², because both are (a number) × r². Twenty seconds, no π, no formula.

B · A cut exposes new faces — count them (2024-U Q51)

Slice any solid with one plane and you create two new flat faces of equal area; the curved and original flat surfaces are unchanged in total. So total surface area of the pieces = original total + 2 × (area of the cut face). Everything is in knowing the cut face's shape:

  • Sphere, plane at distance x from the centre → a circle of radius √(R² − x²) (Pythagoras in the figure below).
  • Cylinder cut along its axis → a rectangle 2r × h; cut across (perpendicular to the axis) → a circle πr².
  • Cube cut parallel to a face → a square a²; cuboid → the matching rectangle.
  • Cone cut through its axis → a triangle ½ × 2r × h = rh.

2024-U Q51 then reads itself: the ball's surface is 4π × 14² = 784π; "3/28 more" is 84π; two circles give 2π(196 − x²) = 84π → 196 − x² = 42 → x² = 154, and since 12² = 144 and 12.5² = 156.25, x ≈ 12.4 cm. Notice that π cancelled and 22/7 was never needed. Counting the cut as one circle gives 196 − x² = 84, x ≈ 10.6 — not an option, so that slip sends you to a guess rather than to a wrong bubble.

C · Three solids on one base (2018-S Q54)

"A cone, a hemisphere and a cylinder stand on equal bases of radius r and have equal heights." A hemisphere's height is its radius, so h = r for all three — that is the hidden step. Then:

  • Volumes 1 : 2 : 3 — always, when h = r.
  • Cone's slant height l = √(r² + r²) = r√2, so its curved surface is πr · r√2 = √2 πr² and its total is πr²(√2 + 1).
  • Totals (√2 + 1) : 3 : 4. The distractor (√3 + 1) : 3 : 4 is a slant height of r√3, i.e. h = r√2 — a wrong hidden step.

E · Recast, pour, wrap — which quantity is shared

  • Melted and recast: volumes equal, and π cancels on both sides. A cone of height 12 and radius 3 recast into a sphere: ⅓π · 9 · 12 = 36π = (4/3)πR³ → R³ = 27 → R = 3. Forgetting the ⅓ in the cone gives R³ = 81 and an untidy 4.33 — untidiness is the warning: recast questions almost always land on integers. (Recasting is worded in NCERT's Class 10 chapter, not the Class 9 one, but it needs nothing beyond the Class 9 volumes; Allen's subject-level items use it.)
  • Tent, pipe, wrapper: curved surface only; a cone's needs l from Pythagoras first.
  • Painting, plastering, tin-plating a solid: total surface. An open tank: total minus the top.
  • Water level in a tank: volume, then divide by the base area.

5Hand arithmetic — the part IGCSE never made you do

No calculator, no log tables, rough work on the booklet. Three habits cover every item above.

  • Keep π as a symbol until the end. In a ratio it cancels; in a recast item it cancels; in the 2024-U cut item it cancelled. Use 22/7 only when a number is demanded — and then hope r is a multiple of 7: (22/7) × 7² = 154, (22/7) × 14 = 44, 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 20 = 880.
  • Surds: pair the primes, never multiply out first. √3456: 3456 = 2⁷ · 3³ → three 2-pairs and one 3-pair come out (2³ · 3 = 24), 2 · 3 = 6 stays: 24√6. Know √2 ≈ 1.414, √3 ≈ 1.732, √5 ≈ 2.236, √6 ≈ 2.449, √7 ≈ 2.646 — but use the value Allen prints when it prints one.
  • Squares to 25 and cubes to 12. 11² 121 · 12² 144 · 13² 169 · 14² 196 · 15² 225 · 16² 256 · 17² 289 · 18² 324 · 19² 361 · 20² 400 · 21² 441 · 22² 484 · 23² 529 · 24² 576 · 25² 625; cubes 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000, 1331, 1728. A recast answer is almost always one of these.

7Five worked questions — at TALLENTEX pitch

Try each before opening the working and give yourself 90 seconds — the real paper's average. The elimination reasoning is written out because on the day, ruling two options out is worth almost as much as solving.

12017-E Q53 and 2018-S Q53 in one chain · ~90 seconds

The sides of a triangular field in a village are in the ratio 13 : 14 : 15 and its perimeter is 84 m. The length of the altitude drawn to the middle side is

112 m
222.4 m
324 m
425.8 m
Working

Step 1 — the sides. 13 + 14 + 15 = 42, so k = 84/42 = 2: sides 26, 28, 30 m. Not a Pythagorean triple (26² + 28² = 676 + 784 = 1460 ≠ 900), so Heron it is.

Step 2 — Heron. s = 42; brackets 16, 14, 12 (sum 42 ✓). Product 42 × 16 × 14 × 12: as primes (2·3·7)(2⁴)(2·7)(2²·3) = 2⁸ · 3² · 7² — every prime paired, so the root is exact: 2⁴ × 3 × 7 = 336 m².

Step 3 — the altitude. The middle side is 28 m: h = 2 × 336 / 28 = 24 m. Option (3).

Where each wrong option comes from. (1) 12 — 336/28, the 2 in h = 2A/b forgotten. (2) 22.4 — 672/30, the altitude to the longest side. (4) 25.8 — 672/26, the altitude to the shortest side. Elimination. Two options are the other two altitudes, so the moment you have the area, only "which side did it name" is left. The 13-14-15 triangle with area 84 is a classic setter's triangle (all three altitudes rational); its double, 26-28-30, keeps that property.

Step 1 — find x by testing, not solving. Heron gives s = 3x/2 and brackets x/2 + 4, x/2 − 4, x/2, so Area² = 3x²(x² − 64)/16 = 96² = 9216, i.e. x²(x² − 64) = 49152 — a quadratic in x², off the list. 3The 2024-U Q51 machine on a cylinder · ~90 seconds

A solid cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 20 cm is cut into two equal halves by a plane through its axis. The total surface area of the two halves together exceeds the total surface area of the original cylinder by (take π = 22/7)

1560 cm²
2880 cm²
31188 cm²
41440 cm²
Working

Step 1 — what the cut creates. A plane through the axis exposes a rectangle on each half, of width 2r = 14 cm and height h = 20 cm. Two of them: 2 × 14 × 20 = 560 cm².

Step 2 — nothing else changes. The curved surface (880 cm²) and the two end circles (154 cm² each) are shared between the halves, not added to. So the increase is exactly the new faces: 560 cm², option (1) — and the 22/7 was a decoy; no π enters the answer at all.

Where each wrong option comes from. (2) 880 — 2πrh, the curved surface, from thinking the cut "adds curved area". (3) 1188 — the whole original TSA, 2πr(r + h) = 44 × 27, offered to anyone who computed the cylinder instead of the cut. (4) 1440 — 560 + 880, the new faces plus the curved surface counted again. Elimination. The answer must be π-free (two rectangles), so any option that is a multiple of 22 with a 7 cancelled — 880, 1188 — is suspect before you compute; 1440 = 880 + 560 is a sum of the other two.
4Multi-statement · the 2018-S Q54 set-up, four clauses · ~2 minutes

A cone, a hemisphere and a cylinder stand on equal bases of radius r and have equal heights. Consider:

  1. Their volumes are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3.
  2. Their curved surface areas are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 2.
  3. The slant height of the cone is r√2.
  4. Their total surface areas are in the ratio (√2 + 1) : 3 : 4.

Which of the statements are correct?

1I and III only
2I, II and IV only
3I, II, III and IV
4I, III and IV only
Working

The hidden step first: a hemisphere's height is r, so all three have h = r.

I — ⅓πr²·r : ⅔πr³ : πr²·r = ⅓ : ⅔ : 1 = 1 : 2 : 3. True.

III — l = √(r² + h²) = √(2r²) = r√2. True.

II — curved: cone πrl = √2 πr²; hemisphere 2πr²; cylinder 2πr·r = 2πr². So √2 : 2 : 2, which is 1 : √2 : √2, not 1 : 2 : 2. False — 1 : 2 : 2 is what you get by putting h in place of l.

IV — totals: cone πr(l + r) = πr²(√2 + 1); hemisphere 3πr²; cylinder 2πr(r + r) = 4πr². Ratio (√2 + 1) : 3 : 4. True — and it is 2018-S Q54's key.

I, III and IV: option (4).

Elimination. III is a one-line Pythagoras and it is true, which kills (2) at once. Then the whole item rests on II: if you write πrh for the cone's curved surface you "confirm" 1 : 2 : 2 and pick (3). The slant height is the entire trap — the same one the words table in section 3 flags. Pitch note: four clauses, one of them false, is exactly the 2024-U Q36 shape that rule 5 of the strategy page warns about — one misread clause swings 5 marks.
5Recast (Class 10 wording, Class 9 arithmetic), then one more step · ~90 seconds

A solid metal hemisphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a right circular cone with the same base radius. The slant height of the cone is

16√2 cm
212 cm
36√5 cm
46√17 cm
Working

Step 1 — volumes equal. Hemisphere: ⅔ π 6³ = ⅔ × 216 π = 144π cm³. Cone: ⅓ π 6² h = 12πh. So 12πh = 144π → h = 12 cm. (π cancelled; 22/7 was never needed.)

Step 2 — the slant height. l² = r² + h² = 36 + 144 = 180 = 36 × 5 → l = 6√5 cm ≈ 13.4. Option (3).

Where each wrong option comes from. (2) 12 — the height, offered to anyone who stops one step early. (4) 6√17 — the whole sphere's volume (4/3 π 6³ = 288π) used for the hemisphere: h = 24, l² = 36 + 576 = 612 = 36 × 17. (1) 6√2 — assuming the cone keeps the hemisphere's height 6: l² = 36 + 36. Elimination. The answer is a slant height, so it must exceed both r = 6 and h; anything ≤ 12 is out once h = 12 is found, which removes (1) and (2) together. Between (3) and (4) it is only whether you remembered the hemisphere's ⅔.
